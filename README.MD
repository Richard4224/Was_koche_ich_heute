# Was koche ich heute?

#### Video Demo: <URL HERE>

#### Description:

*"What shall I cook today?"*: a German recipe website that suggests what to cook and, with a wink, asks the stars for advice.

You open the page and find yourself in space, looking at the moon in its real, current phase. Underneath it you read the signs of the sky: the moon phase, the season and the live weather where you are. When you scroll down, you descend through a starry night sky, past a plane and a flock of birds, towards mountains and a distant city glowing on the horizon, and into a forest of giant firs. Down on the forest floor stands a little stone cottage with glowing windows and a smoking chimney: someone is cooking. Next to it are the recipe book and the *dish of the day*.

My CS50x final project. I also want to use it at home, which is why everything a visitor sees is in German.

## What it can do

- **Signs of the sky:** the current moon phase (drawn as an actual moon), the astronomical season and the live weather with "feels like" temperature and place name.
- **Your location, if you allow it:** the browser shares its position. If it doesn't (or times out), the server guesses the city from the IP address, and if that fails too, it falls back to Berlin. The season flips on the southern hemisphere.
- **318 recipes** in an SQLite database, each with a short description, ingredients, amounts, time, season, detailed step-by-step instructions (heat, timing, how to tell when it's done) and usually a tip.
- **Recipe pages with adjustable servings:** all amounts are recalculated, including amounts mentioned inside the instructions, and shown in kitchen-friendly form (`¼ Zwiebel`, `1,2 kg`, `2 Prisen`, `3 Tage 3 Std.`). The whole photo sits next to the title, and badges show at a glance whether a dish is vegan, vegetarian or gluten-free.
- **Search and filters** that update the results while you type: by name, vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free, by what you have at home and by what you don't want. Pick your ingredients and the recipes that miss the fewest come first, each card saying what's missing ("Es fehlt: Sahne") or "Alles da".
- **A quick bar of main ingredients** ("Worauf hast du Lust?"): Geflügel, Rind & Kalb, Schwein, Fisch, Ei, Käse, Tofu, Pilze, Kartoffeln, Nudeln, Reis, … One click for "with", the minus next to it for "without".
- **Results scroll in their own box** and load more as you reach its end, so the page stays where it is.
- **Coming back from a recipe** returns you to the same search, scrolled to the same spot.
- **Photos from Wikimedia Commons and Pixabay,** each credited under the photo and on the page *Bildnachweise*.
- **Contact form, Impressum and privacy policy,** ready for hosting: the form sends messages by email through [FormSubmit](https://formsubmit.co).
- **A dish of the day** chosen by the season and the holidays, the time of day, the weather and the moon, with the reason on the card ("Ein Herbstgericht zum Abendessen. Wärmt bei 8 °C und ist rund wie der Vollmond.").
- **Works on phones:** the layout adapts, and visitors who prefer reduced motion get a still picture.

- Maybe a German/English switch.

## Getting started

You need Python 3 (developed with Python 3.14 and Flask 3.1).

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m flask run
```

Then open http://127.0.0.1:5000.

- **The database builds itself** from `schema.sql`, `seed.sql` and `images.sql`: on first use, and again whenever one of them is newer than `recipes.db` (after editing the recipes or a `git pull`). The database is in `.gitignore`.
- **Settings** go into a file `.env` (copy `.env.example`; it is not committed). All are optional:
  - `FORMSUBMIT_TO`: the contact form goes through FormSubmit to the address in the Impressum. The very first message makes FormSubmit send an "Activate Form" email; click it, and from then on messages arrive. FormSubmit then also sends a random alias for the address, which can go here so the address itself isn't sent along.
  - `PROXY_COUNT`: see hosting below.
- **Location:** browsers only share the location on `localhost` or over HTTPS. If you block it, the IP fallback described above kicks in.
- **The scenery** (fir rows, cottage, horizon with the city) is generated by a script. To change it, edit and run:
  ```bash
  python tools/generate_scenery.py
  ```
- **Photos** are chosen in two steps. The first searches for candidates and builds a page to pick them (`tools/images/review.html`), the second downloads the picked ones and writes their credits to `images.sql`:
  ```bash
  pip install -r tools/requirements.txt
  python tools/find_images.py
  python tools/fetch_images.py path/to/bildauswahl.json
  ```
  Both only look at recipes that don't have a credited photo yet (`--all` for every recipe). `--pixabay` searches Pixabay instead of Commons; it needs a free API key (log in, then it's shown on [pixabay.com/api/docs](https://pixabay.com/api/docs/)) in `tools/images/pixabay-key.txt` (or the environment variable `PIXABAY_API_KEY`).

## Hosting

The app needs no paid service. The plan is a Raspberry Pi at home with a free [DuckDNS](https://www.duckdns.org) address:

1. **Address:** create a subdomain on DuckDNS (e.g. `waskocheichheute.duckdns.org`) and keep it pointing at your home connection with a cron job on the Pi: `*/5 * * * * curl -s "https://www.duckdns.org/update?domains=NAME&token=TOKEN&ip=" >/dev/null`. In the router, forward ports 80 and 443 to the Pi. This needs a public IPv4 address; with DS-Lite (common with cable providers) incoming connections over IPv4 don't work.
2. **App:** clone the repository to `/home/pi/recipe-search`, then `python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt`, fill in `.env` with `PROXY_COUNT=1`, and install `deploy/recipe-search.service`, which runs it with gunicorn.
3. **HTTPS:** install [Caddy](https://caddyserver.com) and use `deploy/Caddyfile` with your address. Caddy gets the certificate by itself. Browsers only share the location over HTTPS, so this matters for the weather.
4. **Updates:** `git pull` and `sudo systemctl restart recipe-search`; the database rebuilds itself.

The app keeps nothing that has to survive a restart (the database is rebuilt from the SQL files), so a free hosting service with a temporary file system would work too. Check that it allows outgoing requests to Open-Meteo, Nominatim, ipinfo.io and FormSubmit, and set `PROXY_COUNT` to its number of proxies.

## Design decisions

### Data

- **Three tables:** `recipes`, `ingredients` and `recipe_ingredients` in between, because a recipe has many ingredients and an ingredient appears in many recipes. Storing ingredients as text in the recipe would make filtering by ingredient impossible ("Zwiebel" vs. "Zwiebeln").
- **Recipe and ingredient names are unique.** Every ingredient exists exactly once, which is what filtering by ingredient relies on. It also lets `seed.sql` link recipes and ingredients by name instead of remembering ids.
- **Every recipe has a meal type** (breakfast, main, soup, salad, cake, dessert, snack, …), says whether it's eaten warm, and may belong to an occasion (Christmas, New Year's Eve, Easter, Halloween, Oktoberfest). Like the categories, these are set at the end of `seed.sql`.
- **Every ingredient has a category** (spice, oil, pantry, vegetable, poultry, beef, …), set at the end of `seed.sql`. Spices, oils and pantry staples (flour, sugar, onions, garlic, broth, vinegar, …) are assumed to be in every kitchen; the main ingredient categories make up the quick bar.
- **Vegan, vegetarian and gluten-free are stored per ingredient,** not per recipe. A recipe is vegan if all of its ingredients are. One deliberate call: Parmesan and Pecorino count as not vegetarian (animal rennet).
- **Amounts are numbers** (`REAL`), always for the recipe's base `servings`, so they can be scaled. `NULL` means "to taste" (salt, pepper, …).
- **Amounts inside the instructions are placeholders** like `{100 g}` or `{2-3 EL}`, so "add 100 g flour" scales along with the ingredient list.
- **Instructions are plain text with a little structure:** numbered lines are steps, a line starting with `Tipp:` becomes the tip box, and the rest ("Guten Appetit!") is the closing line.
- **Diet badges are worked out, not stored:** a recipe is vegan, vegetarian or gluten-free if all its ingredients are, the same rule the search filters use. Vegan already implies vegetarian, so only "Vegan" is shown then.
- **Servings are whole numbers,** so a cake for one springform scales to 2 springforms, never 1.5.
- **Foreign keys are switched on** (`PRAGMA foreign_keys = ON`) for every connection. SQLite doesn't check them by default.
- **Python's built-in `sqlite3`** instead of the CS50 library, so the project needs nothing beyond Flask.

### Search and filters

- **One search, two ways in.** The start page reads the search from the URL (`/?q=suppe&vegan=1&zutat=Zwiebel`), so it works without JavaScript and survives a reload. With JavaScript, `/recipes` returns just the cards and the page swaps them in while you type, keeping the URL up to date.
- **Case doesn't matter, umlauts included.** SQLite's `LIKE` only ignores case for A–Z, so the app registers Python's `str.casefold` as an SQL function. `%` and `_` are escaped, so they are searched for literally.
- **Diets are checked in SQL:** a recipe is vegan if there is no ingredient in it that isn't (`NOT EXISTS`).
- **"What do I have at home?" ranks instead of excludes.** Requiring every ingredient would almost always find nothing, so all recipes using at least one of your ingredients are found, sorted by how many ingredients are missing. Spices, oils, pantry staples and anything "to taste" (no amount) never count as missing; you can open the full list below the field.
- **Excluding ingredients is strict:** a recipe containing any of them is left out (`NOT EXISTS … IN (…)`). An ingredient can be either "have" or "without", never both; choosing one takes it out of the other.
- **Ingredients are picked from the list of all 352,** so only names that really exist end up in the search. Typing "Ei" on the way to "Eigelb" doesn't add "Ei" too early: only choosing a suggestion or pressing Enter adds one.
- **The quick bar is about the main ingredient,** because that's usually the question: "something with chicken", "rice", "no pork". "With" groups must all be in the recipe (chicken *and* rice); "without" groups must not appear at all. Its links work without JavaScript too.
- **12 results at a time in a scrolling box.** Reaching its end loads the next 12; `overscroll-behavior: contain` keeps the page from scrolling along. How many are shown is kept in the URL, and the scroll position inside the box is remembered, so coming back from a recipe you land on the same card.

### The dish of the day

Every recipe gets points; the one with the most wins. What counts, from most to least:

| | Weight | |
|---|---|---|
| Season | 4 | Dishes of the current season most, "all year" ones somewhat, the neighbouring seasons a little. On occasions, their dishes win: cookies in Advent, raclette on New Year's Eve, Grüne Soße from Maundy Thursday (Easter is calculated for every year), pumpkin before Halloween, Wiesn-Hendl during Oktoberfest. |
| Time of day | 3 | Breakfast (5–10), lunch, coffee and cake (14–17), dinner (17–22), late-night snack. Dishes that don't suit the time at all lose 5 points, so no roast for breakfast. |
| Weather | 2 | Cold: something warm. Rain or snow: soups and stews. Warm and dry: the grill. Hot: something cold and fresh. Judged in bands (frosty, cold, mild, warm, hot), so half a degree doesn't change the dish. |
| Moon | 1 | Full moon: something round (pancakes, pizza, dumplings, cakes). New moon: something dark (chocolate, beetroot, black lentils). Waxing: something hearty to gather strength. Waning: something light. |

- **Deterministic, but not boring.** Two "dice" calculated from the date and the recipe (SHA-256, not random) pick among similar dishes: one changes daily, one yearly, so each year favours different dishes. The same day, time of day and weather always give the same dish, so reloading doesn't change it.
- **No repeats within a week** at the same time of day. Which dish a day gets depends on the days before, so the days of a year are worked out one after another from 1 January (without weather, since past weather is unknown) and remembered: the first request takes well under a second, every later day a few milliseconds.
- **The page appears at once, the weather follows.** The server picks the dish by season, time and moon; when the browser has the weather for its location, `/weather` answers with the dish for that weather and the browser's own clock, and the card fades over if a different dish fits better.

### Moon, season, weather

- **Moon phase:** calculated from a known new moon (6 January 2000) and the length of a lunar cycle (29.53 days), split into 8 phases. Accurate to about one day, which is enough for a page that asks the stars for advice.
- **Season:** astronomical (spring begins around 20 March), using fixed dates that can be a day off in some years. South of the equator it is flipped.
- **Weather:** from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com), which needs no API key. Results are cached for 15 minutes per location (rounded to about 1 km) so not every page load asks the API.
- **Place names:** from OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, which asks every app to identify itself, so requests carry a user agent with this repository's URL.
- **The page loads first, the weather follows.** The browser's location can take a few seconds, so the start page shows "Die Sterne befragen das Wetter …" and fills in the weather via JavaScript.

### Photos

I didn't want to take 300 photos myself or have them generated, so the photos come from Wikimedia Commons, and where it has nothing fitting, from Pixabay.

- **Only licences that allow this use:** public domain, CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA. Not "non-commercial" (NC) or "no derivatives" (ND), and not GFDL alone, which wants the whole licence text printed next to the photo. The licence is checked again when a photo is downloaded.
- **Found by a script, chosen by me.** `find_images.py` searches Commons for the recipe's name. Most photos there are described in English, so "Bratapfel" alone finds little; the script then asks the German Wikipedia, takes the photo of the matching article and searches again with its English title ("Baked apple"), and also tries the dish without its sides ("Lammkeule" for "Lammkeule mit Rosmarin und Knoblauch"). Those hits are often right and sometimes a painting or the elder bush instead of the fritters, so the results go to a page where I pick one per recipe (the first is pre-selected) or none.
- **Pixabay fills the gaps.** Commons photos are mostly people's own home cooking, which suits a cottage in the woods, but for dishes like "Ofen-Feta-Pasta" or "Champignon-Cremesuppe" it has nothing. Pixabay has stock food photography, understands German searches, has a food category, and its licence allows using the photos freely. (Pexels would have been the first choice, but it wasn't issuing new API keys.) Pixabay doesn't allow hotlinking its photos for good, which suits this site anyway: like all photos, they are downloaded and served from `static/images/`.
- **Credits are data.** Author, licence, link to the licence and the photo's page on Commons or Pixabay are stored with the recipe and shown under the photo and on *Bildnachweise*. They live in their own file, `images.sql`, so redoing the photos never touches the recipes in `seed.sql`.
- **Web size:** 960 pixels wide at JPEG quality 75, about 100 KB each, so a few hundred photos fit in the repository.

### Privacy

The site shouldn't lose a feature for privacy, but it shouldn't give away more than it needs either.

- **Nothing from third parties in the browser.** Fonts and Bootstrap are served by the app itself (Bootstrap's JavaScript wasn't used, so it's gone), and so are the photos. A visitor's browser only ever talks to this server.
- **The server asks the APIs, not the browser,** so Open-Meteo and Nominatim never see a visitor's IP address. The browser rounds its location to two decimals (about 1 km) before sending it, which is all the weather needs.
- **The IP fallback uses a shortened address:** the last block of an IPv4 address (everything after the first 48 bits of IPv6) is cut off before it goes to ipinfo.io. That still finds the city, but no longer points at one connection.
- **No cookies, no accounts, no logs of visitors.** The only thing kept in the browser is the "back to my search" spot, in `sessionStorage`. gunicorn and Caddy run without access logs. Caches only live in memory.
- **The contact form goes through the server.** FormSubmit delivers the messages, so no mail password has to live on the server, but the form posts to the app, and the app passes the message on. That way FormSubmit never sees the visitor's IP address, and the app can check the message first: a hidden field that only bots fill in, and at most 3 messages an hour per (shortened) address and 30 a day in all, so the mailbox can't be flooded.
- **German time:** the dish of the day and the season follow `Europe/Berlin`, whatever clock the server runs on.

### Look and feel

The vibe I wanted: mysterious and enchanted, a moonlit night in a forest lit by lanterns, a bit like Zelda's Lost Woods with a hint of the colourful fantasy of *No Game No Life*. Deliberately not a run-of-the-mill recipe site.

- **The start page is a journey,** and the content follows it: the sky shows the signs of the sky, and the cottage, where someone is cooking, is where you find the recipes.
- **Colours:** night blue and purple, warm lantern amber for anything cooking-related (amounts, times, buttons), and a little cyan and lavender for magic.
- **Glass panels** let the scenery shine through behind the content.
- **Fonts:** Fraunces for headings (a soft serif; an earlier, pointier font didn't fit) and Source Sans 3 for text.
- **Generated scenery:** firs, the stones and thatch of the cottage, the gnarled tree and the city are generated with fixed random seeds. That makes them look natural instead of copy-pasted, while every run produces the same result. Each row of firs repeats at the same spacing, with the far row shifted by half a gap so every far tree stands between two close ones.
- **Recipe pages stay calm:** a simple night sky. That's where you cook, not explore.

### Code

- **Content in German, code in English:** everything visitors see is German; names, comments and this README are English.
- **Templates only display.** Rounding, formatting and scaling happen in Python, so templates contain almost no logic.
